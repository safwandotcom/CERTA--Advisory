# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary reader: finance decision-makers (finance directors, controllers, practice owners, CFOs) at mid-to-large companies and accounting practices in the UK, USA, Canada, and Europe who are evaluating (or already use) an accounting outsourcing partner. They are risk-aware and comparison-shopping — likely have 2-3 other vendor sites open in other tabs, including firms like ACE Advisory, RBA Chartered Accountants, and RCI Global. They are scanning for evidence of accounting-specific rigor — qualifications, software fluency, jurisdictional compliance knowledge — fast, not reading marketing copy end-to-end. Secondary reader: SME owners and accounting practice principals exploring outsourcing for the first time, who need more plain-language reassurance and clearer next steps.

## Product Purpose

CERTA& Advisory is a Bangladesh-based outsourcing firm providing accounting-specific services — bookkeeping, management accounts, payroll, VAT/tax compliance, and year-end accounts — to companies and accounting practices in the UK, Canada, USA, and Europe. This is an accounting BPO, not a general-purpose BPO: it does not offer HR, EOR, or unrelated business-process outsourcing, and the site must not imply otherwise. The site's job is to convert a skeptical, comparison-shopping visitor into a discovery call — by establishing accounting-specific credibility fast (qualifications, software platforms, jurisdictional familiarity) and making the service scope and engagement process legible within seconds, not paragraphs.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **Assured, Sharp, Credible.**

- Assured — the name itself signals certainty ("Certa"); the brand should read as calm competence, not eager sales pitch.
- Sharp — modern, precise, editorial — never soft or decorative. Confidence expressed through restraint and clarity, not stock-photo warmth.
- Credible — this is an accounting firm's back office, not a generic outsourcing vendor. Every claim should be backed by something concrete: a qualification, a software platform, a jurisdiction, a number — never a vague adjective.

## Anti-references

Explicitly avoid the visual territory of the three competitor sites reviewed (ACE Advisory, RBA Chartered Accountants, RCI Global):
- Dense, deeply-nested navigation menus that bury the actual offer
- Navy-and-gray "safe corporate" palettes with no distinct brand color presence
- Dated layout execution (cramped sections, inconsistent spacing, low-contrast body text)
- Generic stock-photo hero imagery standing in for an actual point of view

Also avoid, specific to positioning:
- Reading as a generalist BPO (HR/EOR, "any back-office function") rather than an accounting specialist — every service listed must be an accounting-firm function
- Playful startup affectations (full-pill buttons, casual copy) that undercut accounting-firm credibility

CERTA& should look distinctly newer and more considered than the competitor references while reading as serious enough to hold a finance/accounting conversation — closer to "a modern accounting practice's back office" than "a startup's marketing site."

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over density** — one clear idea per section; resist the competitor habit of cramming service lists into dense grids.
2. **Earn trust through precision, not decoration** — credibility comes from clear process/scope language and confident typography, not badges or stock photography.
3. **Brand color with intent** — the green + coral from the logo mark should appear as deliberate, restrained accents against a clean neutral field, not diluted into generic "corporate blue."
4. **Global-first legibility** — content and layout should read as credible to a UK/US/EU reader with zero local-market friction (currency, spelling, time zone references handled neutrally).
5. **Show the process, not just the promise** — decision-makers are de-risking a vendor choice; make engagement steps and scope concrete wherever possible.
6. **Concrete accounting credibility over generic reassurance** — lead with qualifications, named software platforms (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, FreeAgent), and jurisdictional specifics (VAT, GAAP/IFRS, statutory deadlines) instead of adjectives like "trusted" or "reliable."
7. **Accounting-only scope discipline** — never imply HR, EOR, or general BPO services. If a section could apply to any outsourcing vendor rather than specifically an accounting one, rewrite it.
8. **Full scale, not full noise** — the site should read as a firm operating at real scale (full-bleed layout, a confident hero, considered scroll motion on two signature visuals) without tipping into a flashy web-agency register. Every borrowed pattern gets filtered through "would an accounting firm's compliance officer be comfortable putting their name on this."
9. **Honest team/client representation** — Team, Clients, and Careers content must never fabricate specific named people, credentials, or client logos that don't exist. Use role-based structure and sector/market profiles instead; swap in real names, photos, and logos only once the user supplies them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the baseline: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text/headings ≥3:1, visible focus states on all interactive elements, `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for any motion. No additional accessibility requirements specified.
